# 6. Discussion

In this study, we introduced a comprehensive, hierarchical framework for offline Bangla writer identification, capable of executing verification, retrieval, unsupervised clustering, and multi-author composition segmentation. By benchmarking our custom Dual-Path Patch Encoder (DPE-Net) against the state-of-the-art FasterNet-T0, we established clear operational parameters for both high accuracy and low latency. Our findings demonstrate that the pretrained FasterNet-T0 serves as a formidable, high-accuracy baseline, achieving near-perfect metrics across page-level verification (96.00%) and retrieval (100.00% Top-1 Accuracy). Concurrently, our custom DPE-Net proved exceptionally competitive while fulfilling the mandate for being lightweight. Despite operating with only a fraction of the parameters (0.1775 M vs. 3.27 M), DPE-Net maintained robust biometric rigor, securing a 95.00% page-level verification accuracy and a 94.20% Top-1 retrieval rate. 

The most significant advantage of DPE-Net lies in its computational footprint. Training the DPE-Net architecture for 46 epochs required only ~13 hours, whereas FasterNet-T0 demanded approximately 34.5 hours of continuous training in our hardware settings. During real-world inference, DPE-Net completed the end-to-end processing (preprocessing, encoding, inference) of a full handwritten document in just 0.5163 seconds, edging out FasterNet-T0's 0.5969 seconds. This confirms DPE-Net's viability for real-time, large-scale document screening in edge devices where computational resources are strictly limited.

Analyzing task-specific behaviors reveals key nuances in how the latent space models stylistic variation. Specifically, within writer-based document clustering, the agglomerative thresholds were strictly tuned to maximize pairwise accuracy—prioritizing absolute cluster purity over generalized grouping. As a result, the algorithm demonstrated high sensitivity to intra-writer variance. Both models over-segmented the 12 ground-truth evaluation writers (forming 19 clusters for DPE-Net and 22 for FasterNet-T0) while maintaining pairwise accuracies above 96%. This indicates a conservative algorithmic bias that enforces strict morphological consistency; natural stylistic shifts (e.g., varying pens or physical writing conditions) are safely isolated into distinct identities. Consequently, this strict partitioning maximizes True Negative rates, effectively safeguarding against the erroneous merging of different authors.

Furthermore, expanding the analysis from isolated document grouping to sequential multi-writer segmentation revealed a distinct performance trade-off in chronological reconstruction. During the multi-author page composition evaluation, FasterNet-T0 achieved a higher Absolute Sequence Accuracy (79.38% vs. 75.26%) but recorded a higher Sequence Error Rate (0.1168 vs. 0.0868) compared to DPE-Net. This metric inversion indicates that while FasterNet-T0 flawlessly reconstructs a greater volume of pages, its occasional misclassifications yield severe Levenshtein edit distances. Conversely, DPE-Net exhibits lower overall edit-distance variance. Ultimately, FasterNet-T0 maximizes absolute precision for flawless sequence recovery, whereas DPE-Net provides a highly stable, tightly bounded error margin across complex layouts.
